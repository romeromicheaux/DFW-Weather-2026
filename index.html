What does all this mean?

ACTUAL AIR TEMPERATURE is measured by official weather stations under standardized conditions. Instruments are shielded from direct sunlight and properly ventilated.

FEELS LIKE estimates how the weather may feel to the human body.

DEW POINT describes how much moisture is in the air. On hot days, higher dew points can make it harder for sweat to evaporate efficiently, making hot weather feel even hotter.

A very hot day does not automatically create a big difference. For example, a daily average of 100°F actual and 103°F feels-like would appear as only +3°F here.

Why might my car show a different temperature?

Your car’s outside-temperature sensor sits in a different environment from an official weather station. Hot pavement, nearby surfaces, the vehicle itself, traffic and direct solar heating can influence its reading. A car showing 113°F while an official station reports 101°F does not necessarily mean either reading is defective: they are measured under different conditions.

How to read these numbers

Feels-like difference = feels-like temperature minus measured temperature. Monthly and dew point averages give every observation equal weight. Rain comparisons give every day equal weight after averaging its observations. Rankings use daily observation averages. Reporting times vary, so these are not time-weighted averages. Days follow the timestamps supplied; timezone is unspecified. Associations do not establish causes. The reported feels-like values may be calculated, rather than independently measured.
